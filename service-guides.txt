How Dryer Vent Cleaning Works: A Step-by-Step Guide for Property Managers 
Meta Title: How Dryer Vent Cleaning Works | Air Tech Solutions 
Meta Description: A step-by-step look at how professional dryer vent cleaning works, how long it takes, and what property managers can expect. 
Dryer vents are easy to forget about, right up until a clogged one causes a fire, a tenant complaint, or a spike in energy bills. In multi-family buildings, lint builds up faster than most people expect, and clearing it is one of the simplest ways to protect a property. Here is how professional dryer vent cleaning actually works. 
Why It Matters 
Lint is highly flammable, and clogged dryer vents are one of the leading causes of building fires. Beyond safety, a blocked vent forces dryers to run longer, which wastes energy and wears out equipment faster. 
How It Works, Step by Step 
1.  
Inspection. We start by inspecting the dryer, the vent connection, and the exterior vent opening to see how much buildup is present and confirm the full path of the duct. 
2.  
Setup. We protect the area around the dryer and pull the unit out far enough to reach the vent connection without damaging the wall or appliance. 
3.  
Air agitation. We send compressed air through the duct to break lint and debris loose from the walls of the vent, working from the inside out. 
4.  
Vacuum capture. As the lint is loosened, a high-powered vacuum captures it so nothing is left inside the duct or blown into the unit. 
5.  
Exterior check. We confirm the exterior vent flap is clear and moving freely, since a stuck flap is a common hidden cause of poor airflow. 
6. 
Final test. We reconnect the dryer and confirm airflow is strong and the system is venting properly before we move on. 
How Long It Takes 
A single dryer vent usually takes 30 to 45 minutes. For multi-family buildings, we work unit by unit on a schedule that minimizes disruption, and we can often complete a full floor or building section in a day depending on layout and access. 
What to Expect 
Minimal disruption to residents, a clear before-and-after difference in airflow, and documentation you can keep for your records.

Meta Description: Learn how soft washing safely cleans your building's exterior, the step-by step process, and how long the job takes. 
Soft washing is one of the best ways to clean a building's exterior without the risk that comes with blasting it with high pressure. It removes dirt, algae, and mildew at the source and keeps surfaces cleaner for longer. Here is how the process works from start to finish. 
Why It Matters 
Siding, roofing, and finished surfaces can be damaged by high-pressure washing. Soft washing uses low pressure and the right cleaning solutions instead, so your building gets clean without the wear and tear. 
How It Works, Step by Step 
1.  
Walkthrough. We assess the surfaces, identify problem areas like algae or staining, and note anything that needs extra care or protection. 
2.  
Surface prep. We protect landscaping, fixtures, and nearby surfaces that should not be sprayed. 
3.  
Solution application. We apply a cleaning solution at low pressure that breaks down dirt, algae, mold, and mildew at the root. 
4.  
Dwell time. We let the solution sit for a few minutes so it can do the work, rather than relying on force. 
5.  
Rinse. We rinse the surface with low pressure, lifting away the loosened buildup without damaging the material underneath. 
6. 
Final inspection. We check the results and re-treat any spots that need a second pass. 
How Long It Takes 
Most jobs are completed in a single visit. Timing depends on the size of the building and how much surface area needs treatment, but a typical building section takes a few hours. Larger multi-story properties may take a full day or more, and we use our own boom lifts and rope access equipment to reach height safely. 
What to Expect 
A noticeably cleaner exterior, results that last longer than pressure washing, and a crew that works around your residents and tenants.
